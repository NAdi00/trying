1. Job Hunting Tips

  • Apply fast: Applying soon after a job is posted can improve your chances of being noticed by recruiters.
  • Use multiple platforms: Search across platforms such as Indeed, Careers24 and LinkedIn to find more opportunities.
  • Location matters: Make sure your CV clearly shows your location and consider the distance between you and the workplace.
  • Keywords matter: Include relevant keywords from the job advertisement, such as "data capture", "MS Excel" or "filing".
  • Use a professional file name: Save your CV as: Name_Surname_Admin_CV.pdf
  • Follow up: If appropriate, follow up after applying and politely express your interest in the position.

2. CV Writing: Private vs Public Posts

Type What it means CV changes to make
Private Company Businesses such as retail, banking and telecommunications companies. Keep the CV concise. Focus on relevant skills, achievements and experience. Use action words such as "Increased", "Saved" and "Processed".
Government / Public Government departments, municipalities and public institutions. Follow the requirements in the vacancy advertisement carefully and provide all requested documentation.
NGO / NPO Organisations focused on social, community or development objectives. Highlight volunteering, community involvement, relevant skills and your ability to contribute to the organisation's mission.

3. Internship vs Apprenticeship vs Learnership vs Junior Role

Type For who? Qualification Output Pay
Internship Graduates and students gaining practical workplace experience. Usually diploma, degree or relevant qualification, depending on the programme. Practical work experience. Usually a stipend.
Apprenticeship People training for skilled trades such as plumbing, electrical work or mechanical trades. Requirements depend on the trade and programme. Practical training and, where applicable, trade certification. Usually an allowance or wage.
Learnership People who want structured workplace and theoretical training. Requirements vary according to the specific learnership. Workplace experience and an applicable qualification or certificate. Usually a stipend.
Junior Position People starting their professional career. Depends on the employer and position. Employment and professional experience. Salary.
Important: Always read the specific vacancy requirements before applying. Do not remove qualifications from your CV simply because a position is a learnership. If the advertisement has specific eligibility requirements, make sure your application complies with them.

4. Interview Tips That Work

  1. Before: Research the company and understand what they do.
  2. Dress: Wear clean, appropriate and professional clothing.
  3. STAR method: For experience-based questions, structure your answer around Situation, Task, Action and Result.
  4. Weakness question: Mention a genuine weakness and explain what you are doing to improve it.
  5. Ask questions: Prepare questions such as: "What does success look like in the first six months?"
  6. After: Send a short thank-you message after the interview.
Final checklist before you send any application:

1. Does my CV match the requirements of the job?
2. Did I include relevant keywords from the advertisement?
3. Is my email address professional?
4. Did I check spelling and grammar?
5. Did I attach the correct CV and supporting documents?
