Build skills that employers look for

Not every career requires a university degree. South Africa has many vocational, occupational, regulatory and short-course pathways. Before paying a provider, check accreditation, entry requirements, assessment arrangements and whether the employer actually requires that qualification.

6. RE5 — Financial Services

Financial Services

RE5 Regulatory Examination

RE5 is the regulatory examination for representatives in all categories of financial service providers, subject to the FSCA rules and listed exceptions.

Exam fee: check the current legislated fee

The FSCA states that RE5 has 50 multiple-choice questions and a 65% pass mark. Training providers may charge additional preparation-course fees.

7. Job tips — what to do before paying for a course

1. Check accreditation

Ask whether the provider is properly accredited/registered for the qualification or course and which body recognises it.

2. Check the job first

Search real vacancies and write down the exact certificates, licences, experience and software employers request.

3. Compare 3 providers

Compare price, duration, assessment, practical hours, certificate, accreditation and what is included.

4. Don't confuse a certificate with a trade qualification

A short course can teach useful skills but does not automatically make you a qualified artisan or professional.

5. Watch for hidden costs

Ask about registration, exam fees, PPE, transport, materials, medicals, licence tests and certificate fees.

6. Build a skills combination

Example: Forklift + warehouse experience + basic computer/Excel skills can make a stronger logistics application than one certificate alone.

7. Keep evidence

Save certificates, statements of results, licences, ID copies where appropriate, references and a current CV.

8. Never pay for a guaranteed job

Be suspicious of anyone promising employment in exchange for an upfront “job placement” fee.
